<h2>Use active language</h2>
<h3>Active vs passive</h3>
<p>There is no need to define active or passive language. Passive speech may omit the perpetrator, creating ambiguity; therefore, scientific papers should use active language as much as possible. Proactive language can also improve the readability of sentences, especially for long sentences that are common in the paper.</p>
<p>Advantages of the active language:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Emphasis on author ' s responsibilities</strong>（Author&#39;s Responsibility: We / I indicates the author 's responsibility for the text</li>
<li><strong>Enhancing readability</strong>(Make it Readable): Readers are easier to keep up with. Description</li>
<li><strong>Reducing ambiguity</strong>(Reduce Ambiguity): Identifying the dominant language helps us to get our thinking straight.</li>
</ul>
<p>Not all places are used in active language. In general, the Methods part, which is usually performed by We, is not the focus of the operator. Use passive speech to highlight actions and their results and reduce the repetition of We, which will make the structure clearer.</p>
<p>** The use of active language often leads to We and I, which is no problem. ** The paper was designed and completed by a person, and the removal of the pronoun would not make the article more objective.</p>
<p>In the Results and Discussion sections, the active language is usually more appropriate, as both sections place greater emphasis on the author ' s analysis of the results of the experiment.</p>
<p>The content and the Abstract parts do not have a fixed language requirement, which should be determined on a case-by-case basis. The Tables and Figures section is mainly devoted to the active discourse of the study.</p>
<h3>verbs</h3>
<p>As with the active verb, verbs affect reading. The verb drives the sentence forward; turning it into a noun or more complex structure would slow down reading. There are three principles to this effect:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Use strong verbs</strong>(Use strong verbs): The proper verbs can express the meaning more accurately. The first step is to stay away from To be, find more appropriate verbs.</li>
<li><strong>Do not change the verb to a noun</strong>（Don&#39;t turn into nouns: verbs and active verbs expose ambiguity and make the articles easier to understand.</li>

</ul>
<h2>Punctuation, sentence and paragraph</h2>
<h3>Punctuation</h3>
<p>The proper use of other points, in addition to commas and stop-ups, can also organize longer sentences.</p>
<p>By the intensity of the partition, the common points can be classified in order of their intensity:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Comma</strong>（Comma）</li>
<li><strong>Colony</strong>（Colon）</li>
<li><strong>Declining</strong>（Dash）</li>
<li><strong>parenthesis</strong>（Parentheses）</li>
<li><strong>Semicolon</strong>（Semicolon）</li>
<li><strong>Period</strong>（Period）</li>
</ol>
<p>The break marks and semicolons are less formal and should be used less in academic papers; they should be used in a way that improves the narrative structure.</p>
<p><strong>Semicolon</strong>: Short sentences that are similar or counterproductive may be linked by semicolons (;). list item is separated by a semicolon if the list item is long and there are commas in the interior.</p>
<p><strong>parenthesis</strong>: For additional ideas, explanations and details, it may accommodate words, phrases or sentences. Readers can generally skip the bracketed contents; sometimes, interesting content can be added.</p>
<p><strong>Colony</strong>: Often used to explain the concept before it, it can be described or supplemented and emphasised on content. It can also be used to quote others, even in conjunction with semicolons, and to quote them in parallel.</p>
<p><strong>Declining</strong>: A description to insert or highlight. It can be placed almost anywhere, without abuse; it is only used when the insertion is long or when emphasis is necessary. Use a dash to separate the main and core verbs.</p>
<p><strong>Parallel Column Structure</strong>(Parallism): Use and / or but connect sentences with similar structures, often in consecutive steps or parallel objects, as needed. Not all parallels require a hyphen, and a comma can be used directly.</p>
<h3>Paragraph</h3>
<p>Each paragraph should contain only one core idea to reduce the reader ' s reading pressure. Paragraphs should be kept short, with 3-5 sentences as a maximum; the blanks left by the short paragraphs would be useful for reading.</p>
<p>The paragraph should begin with an idea of the paragraph, which the reader can read with and at the end of the paragraph with a kickback.<strong>Paragraphs should be logically expanded.</strong>, the parallel structure could be used appropriately to supplement the information, but not to misuse the transitional term.</p>
<p>Logical development can follow a time sequence, or can move from abstract to specific, or in parallel.</p>
<h2>Writing Process</h2>
<p>Before writing, the experiment was completed and data and preliminary results obtained. Writing Division <strong>Prewriting, preliminary draft, revision</strong> Three phases.</p>
<h3>Prewriting</h3>
<p>During the Prewringing phase, it is important to specify what story the article will tell, with a brief Outline for each of the main parts. Collect and organize the information that may be required (e.g., important references), including experimental data.</p>
<p>The systematic preservation of information that may be used will eliminate the need to rely on external information for final writing, particularly in the general section. Prewriting does not necessarily have to be in front of computers; the same can be said about the structure of articles and the details that may be involved when sports or rest.</p>
<h3>Preliminary draft</h3>
<p>At the Draft stage, the whole concept is translated into a logical structure, leaving the language details behind. Do not balance details with the overall picture, lest both sides fail. Focus on the information organization first, and the sentence is complete.</p>
<h3>Modify</h3>
<p>In the Revise phase, amend item by item as detailed in the previous text. Reading articles helps to identify redundancy and inappropriate structures; checking verbs improve readability. Bold to reduce redundancy and finally check the appropriateness of the logical structure.</p>
<p><strong>Overall inspection</strong>: Important details of the whole text should be consistent, especially key parameters and data. The number is omitted from the multiple rounds of changes. Check the reference to ensure that it supports the conclusions; use the original literature as far as possible and not disseminate secondary references.</p>
<p>Throughout the process, prewriting** is the most important, followed by modifications and finally by the first draft. ** The first draft will naturally be faster when the advance writing is adequate.</p>

<p>The chart and table should also be separated from the body of the text by telling their own story, and abbreviations and laboratory details should be defined. <h3>Results</h3>
<p>The Results section should refrain from using numbers or similar precise descriptions; precise descriptions are required only if the chart is inaccurate or does not address the relevant content. ** Instead of reproducing what is already in the chart, it is intended to explain what the reader understands and to tell the story of the study. **Results can be divided into sub-parts when they are too long.</p>

<h3>Introduction</h3>
<p>The introduction is more clearly formulated. The typical quotation usually has three paragraphs, which are contained between 2 and 5 if necessary. <p>The introductory part should be constructed as one.<strong>The shape of the funnel</strong>(Down down and down):</p>

<li><strong>Existing gaps</strong>: discuss the shortcomings of existing research and the shortcomings. This is the basis for our research objectives or assumptions.</li>
<li><strong>Our approach</strong>: explain the methods we have proposed and how they have filled the gaps in previous studies.</li>
</ul>
<p>For the purpose of generalization, two paragraphs could be used to deal with Known and Unknown, the last paragraph giving the methodology and its implications. You can also cross-reference Known and Unknown, but the study usually ends.</p>
<p>For Known information, it is not necessary to describe the details of each study on a case-by-case basis, and a general statement and a continuous reference can be made.</p>
<p>The introduction need not be too long, but should be easy to understand and not introduce too much technical complexity. The focus is on the proposed methodology and how it can fill existing research gaps. Do not give or imply the result in the introduction: just ask the question, and do not solve it here.</p>
